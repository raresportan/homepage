Subsidized AI inference is slowly becoming a thing of the past. 
It becomes more and more expensive to use the latest models, at least for non-enterprise users. 
Running tons of agents in parallel is not a thing anymore, it's just too expensive. 
Ironically, some start to think that [humans are now cheaper than AI.](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/microsoft-data-suggests-using-ai-225900743.html)

Sadly this means that some "AI-native" companies will have to reduce their headcount and move more money to AI inference. Willy-nilly AI usage will need to fuel their fuzzy fantasy dreams of greatness.
Others, more intellingent, will demand clear ROI from using AI, they will compare the AI bills with the outcomes they got from using that AI.

The same thing can be applied to us, regular John or Jane Doe. We need to see if our AI usage has any good impact. 

And I'm not talking about meaningless metrics like _lines of code generated_ or _number of PRs_, or _productivity increase by 20%_. 
I'm talking about real outcomes, like:
* I found a critical bug that I would have missed without AI
* I had more fun working on this project because AI helped me with the boring stuff
* I learned something new because AI explained it to me in a way that I understood

I call this *"Outcome Oriented AI"*:
> Do not use AI for the sake of using it, use it to help you achieve a practical outcome.

## Why Outcome Oriented AI

One can use AI in a lot of different ways, but not all of them are good. 
In fact most of them are just a waste of time and money because:
1. The same thing could be done cheaper and faster without AI
2. The thing AI made is not the thing you actually wanted
3. The code AI wrote is so bad that you don't have the guts to release it to anyone

**Outcome Oriented AI** is an attempt to avoid these issues: _Decide on an outcome and focus on how to achieve it._

### Clear ROI

First thing you need to do is to define a _realistic_ outcome you want to achieve. 
**Use your experience and intuition** to define a clear path to that outcome. 

If you can't see a clear path, use AI for brainstorming, or for suggesting options / alternatives. 
Use another AI to tear apart those options, and give you feedback on them. 

**Use your judgement** to choose the best option, or to combine them. 
No matter if you succed or fail, it's your decision and responsibility! 
But at least you know what's going on.

Do not proceed with the implementation until you have a clear enough plan to reach that outcome. 
If necessary, break down the outcome into smaller sub-outcomes, and define clear paths to achieve each of them.

> If you don't see a clear way to achieve the outcome, then maybe it's not a good outcome to pursue, or maybe you need to learn more. "$1,000,000 in my bank account" or "be the president of the USA" are not very realistic outcomes for most people.

Here are some realistic outcomes:
* Fix a critical bug in my codebase
* Implement a new feature that I have been wanting to implement for a long time
* Improve the performance of a critical part of my codebase
* Understand what's causing an error
* Understand how a legacy codebase works
* Write documentation for my project
* Migrate codebase to a new version of framework or library
* Write tests for my codebase
* Refactor a messy part of my codebase
* Create a presentation about a technical topic
* Create a prototype to test an idea
* Create internal tools to automate a boring task, to visualize some data, to generate reports, etc.

### Cost savings

I know that is very tempting to just ask the AI to do any silly idea that comes to your mind. 
The dopamine rush of seeing the AI generate something is very addictive. But more often than not, you will end up with a lot of wasted tokens, and a lot of frustration.
Just like playing a slot machine at the casino, you might get some wins, but most of the time you lose.

Now your boss is asking *"Hey, your AI bill is $5,000 this month, can you explain why? What is the business ROI of that?"* This is very different from the *"Just use AI for everything, it's free and it will make you 10x more productive"* mindset that most bosses had just a few months ago.
(Non-local) AI usage is not free! People even start to avoid latest models because they are too expensive.

Just think about it:
* When you have a clear outcome in mind and how to achieve it, you can better estimate the cost of using AI to achieve that outcome.
You can compare that cost with the expected benefits of achieving that outcome, and decide if it's worth it or not.

* When you don't run agents in a loop day and night, you can save a lot of tokens (money.) 

* When you use AI for small clear tasks that have a clear impact, you can save a lot of time and effort simply because the chances of AI to going off the rails are much smaller!

* When you don't use AI for unrealistic or fanciful outcomes, you do yourself a favor.
 
* But when you let the AI build huge codebases you don't understand, you cause yourself and your team a lot of headaches and embarrassment when the codebase inevitably becomes unmaintainable even by AI.
The context required to make a change in that codebase would be huge. AI costs will be huge.

Using AI effectively is a skill that not many people have mastered yet, but just using your common sense and your experience can give you some healthy hints.

> *Life Hack:* Read all the code the AI generates, and make sure you understand it. Do not just blindly accept it. 
This will naturally limit the amount of code you ask the AI to generate and thus the costs associated with it.

### Quality of life improvements

There are some outcomes that are not directly related to money or ROI, but they can still be very valuable. 
They can improve your quality of life. By itself, each outcome might not be a big deal, but they _compound over time and can make a big difference._

How to find small wins?
* Look at your codebase, your workflows. What bothers you? What you always wanted to fix but never had the time? 
* What is the most boring part of your work? 
* What code you need to change but you are afraid of breaking something because you don't understand it?
* What tool is frustrating to use? Or is too slow? Or is too expensive?
* What information is hard to get?
* What kind of reports do you need to create that are time consuming and boring?

Sometimes is worth spending AI tokens to create internal tools that can save you a lot of time and effort in the long run.
Since these tools are not public facing, they don't have to be perfect, they just have to be good enough to help somebody on your team.

Some type of internal tools you can create with AI:
* A private website available on your intranet, that can be used by non-technical people to update various content bits like copy, images, etc. without having to ask the engineering team to do it for them. Or one that generated various reports.
* A Carrd/Wix like tool used by marketing people to make landing pages
* Simple but useful tools like image compressor / image enhancer / html or json validator / csv to json converter / etc. These little tools can save a lot of time and effort.
* Various custom DevTools like benchmarking tools, code/log analyzers, code visualizers, etc. that can help you understand your codebase better and make better decisions or just to integrate in automated workflows.

> **Life Hack:** create all kind of HTML reports, to make them more sharable and interactive.

Everyone uses Markdown files with AI in different ways. 
But not many people use HTML, which is ideal a much better format than Markdown for sharing and/or for information visualization.
Here are some ideas of HTML docs:
- Interactive explainer of code, architecture, workflow, etc. 
- Feature code workflows with code snippets, diagrams, etc.
- Bug cause tracking: analyze Github history for a file/feature to pin point the commit that introduced a bug, and create a report about it, with the code changes, the author, the date, etc. 
- Github repo analysis report, with stats about the codebase, the contributors, the issues, the PRs, etc.
- Standup report, maybe using JIRA or Github PRs, to create nice reports for managers
- Slides/decks for presentations, events, etc.

All these little outcomes are not going to make you rich, but they can make your work more enjoyable, and your life a bit easier.

### Keep control

Pursuing realistic outcomes with a clear path to achieve them is a good way to keep control.
When you decide what needs to be done, and how to do it, you are in control. 

As any programmer will tell you, writing code means taking a lot of small decisions all the time. Writing code implies reading a lot of the existing code.
You need to decide if existing code must be left alone, or if it needs to be refactored to fit the new code. You think of cons and pros of each decision. You think of various options you have. 

When AI writes that code, it is making those decisions for you. You are not aware of those decisions, and don't understand them.
For small changes, this might not be a big deal, but if you don't look at what AI is doing and you just blindly accept it, you go astray.

Look at the code the AI generates, read it, understand it, and make sure it's on the right path. If is not, correct it!

- complexity is the ultimate enemy.
- complexity is bad for humans because they dont unstand it, and they dont know how to use it.
- complexity is bad for AI because they just cant handle it at some point.
